Model Talk (200 words)

200 words

Topic: The impact of technology on learning

Today I’ll talk about how technology changes the way students learn. First of all, it gives instant access to lessons, videos, and practice quizzes, so we can review difficult topics at our own pace. Another advantage is communication. We can discuss homework in group chats, share class notes, and ask teachers questions on school platforms. For example, my class uses a forum to post ideas before projects, which saves time in lessons. However, there are challenges. Notifications and games can distract us, and sometimes we depend on answers instead of thinking carefully. To manage this, it helps to set clear goals, turn off alerts while studying, and use websites that explain steps, not just final answers. Finally, technology supports different learning styles. Visual learners benefit from diagrams, while auditory learners can use podcasts. To sum up, technology is powerful for learning if we plan our time, check reliable sources, and stay focused on understanding, not just finishing tasks.

Perhaps the students are planning homework or activities, because they look relaxed and focused. Overall, the scene feels safe, modern, and friendly, with public transport, clear signs, and spaces where people can meet comfortably.

Model: The Impact of Social Media on Teenagers (180 words)

180 words

Social media is part of daily life for many teenagers, and its impact depends on how it is used. On the positive side, it helps young people stay connected with friends and family, especially when they move schools or live abroad. It also enables students to share notes, discover study tips, and learn about events in their communities.

However, there are risks. Constant notifications can interrupt homework and reduce sleep. Some users compare themselves with carefully edited posts and feel pressure to look perfect. In addition, misinformation spreads quickly, so students must check reliable sources before believing or sharing content.

What can teenagers do? Setting time limits, turning off alerts during study sessions, and curating feeds to include helpful, positive accounts can make a big difference. Schools and parents can support by discussing online wellbeing and teaching digital literacy.

Used wisely, social media can be a useful tool rather than a problem. The key is balance: connect, learn, and create, but protect your focus, health, and confidence.

Report

Model: Student Recycling Habits at School (180 words)

180 words

Introduction: This report summarises recycling habits among Year 10 students and suggests ways to increase participation.

Findings: A short survey indicated that most students recycle paper regularly, while fewer recycle plastic and metal. Many students said bins were sometimes full or placed far from classrooms. Several students were unsure about sorting rules, particularly for drink cartons. Posters were noticed by some learners, but the language was small and the colours were not very clear.

Recommendations: Firstly, increase the number of mixed-recycling bins and position them near busy corridors. Secondly, update labels using simple icons and contrasting colours to reduce confusion. Thirdly, launch a weekly “eco challenge” during tutor time, with small rewards for classes that recycle correctly. Finally, invite the eco-club to give a short presentation in assemblies and answer questions.

Conclusion: With clearer guidance, convenient bins, and regular reminders, students are likely to recycle more materials and develop lasting habits. These steps are low-cost and achievable this term.

3. Summary Writing (180 words, 3 paragraphs)

Source Text

Text: Should Schools Start Later in the Morning?

Many teenagers do not sleep enough during the week. Research suggests later start times can improve attendance, mood, and exam results. When schools begin after 8:45 a.m., students report feeling more alert in lessons and are less likely to be late. In addition, there may be fewer traffic accidents involving young drivers.

However, changing the timetable can be difficult. Parents who start work early may struggle with childcare. After-school clubs could finish later, leaving less time for homework or part-time jobs. Public transport schedules might not match the new times, and bus costs could increase.

Overall, schools need to balance academic benefits with practical challenges. Clear communication with families, careful planning, and trials can help communities decide whether later starts are suitable for them.

Student task: underline key points; paraphrase; write 3 paragraphs: advantages, disadvantages, opinion.

Model Summary (3 paragraphs)

Paragraph 1 – Advantages: The text explains that starting school later can raise attendance, concentration, and examination performance because teenagers get more sleep. Students feel more awake in class and arrive on time more often. A further benefit mentioned is greater road safety for young drivers, as they are less tired in the mornings.

Paragraph 2 – Disadvantages: On the other hand, revised timetables create practical problems. Parents who begin work early may need extra childcare. Clubs could end later, reducing time for homework or jobs. Transport timetables may not fit, and costs might rise if additional buses are required.

Paragraph 3 – Opinion: In my view, later starts are promising but require planning. Schools should consult families, run a short trial, and adjust club times to protect study hours. If transport and childcare are organised clearly, I believe the academic and wellbeing gains will outweigh the difficulties.
